# Goals

**What it answers:** am I on track for the things I said I would do?

A goal has a name, a target date, and a way of measuring progress. Progress appears here and on the
[Dashboard](dashboard.md).

## Kinds

- **Financial** — a number with a target: net worth, a balance, contributions, paying a loan down.
- **Status only** — something that is simply done or not, like "Attend a conference".

## Where "current" comes from

Choose the metric and the app measures it for you rather than asking you to update a number:

| Metric | Measures |
| --- | --- |
| Net worth | Everything owned less owed |
| Account balances | Chosen accounts |
| Contributions into an account type | Money into all accounts of a type |
| 401(k) contributed | Estimated from your paycheck deductions |
| Category inflow / outflow | Spending or income in a category |
| Loan balance | With **lower is better** ticked |
| Manual | You type it |

Hover a row to see the formula and where the figure came from.

**Contributions into an account type** counts deposits *and* purchases, because money reaches a
retirement account by being invested — a 401(k) contribution arrives as a fund purchase, not a
deposit. Reinvested dividends do not count: that is the fund paying itself, not money you put in.

## On track

The bar is progress toward the target; the tick is how much of the time has elapsed. Behind the tick
means behind pace. A **starting value** makes the bar measure progress from where you began rather
than from zero.

## Goals that need time off

A trip, or anything else that takes days off: under **Needs time off from**, pick the time-off bucket,
the hours it needs and the day the time off starts. The editor shows straight away what that bucket will
hold by then — the latest stub's balance carried forward through your pay dates — and whether it's
enough. The Goals list shows the same check under the goal's name, and the dashboard's "What changed
this month" says when you have enough to book it.
